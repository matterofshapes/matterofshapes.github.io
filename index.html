Matter of Shapes

We study how the shape of a domain governs the quantities attached to it, such as eigenvalues, torsional rigidity and perimeter, and which shapes make them as small or as large as possible.

The group is based at the Department of Mathematics and Applications “R. Caccioppoli” of the University of Naples Federico II, with members at the Scuola Superiore Meridionale and at universities in Molise, Milan and Prague.

What we study

Spectral and isoperimetric inequalities

We look for the shapes that make eigenvalues and torsional rigidity as small or as large as possible, and measure how far a domain is from the optimum when equality almost holds.

  • Faber–Krahn and Saint-Venant type inequalities
  • Robin, Neumann and Steklov eigenvalues
  • Bounds of Pólya, Makai, Payne and Weinstock type
  • Quantitative stability
λ1(Ω) ≥ λ1(Ω♯),  |Ω| = |Ω♯|

Symmetrization and comparison

We compare the solution of an elliptic problem with the solution of a symmetrized one, and extend these comparison principles to Robin and Neumann conditions, to new operators and to quantitative forms.

  • Talenti comparison results
  • The Pólya–Szegő principle
  • Schwarz, anisotropic and Hessian symmetrization
  • Rearrangements of the gradient
∫Ω♯ |∇u♯|p ≤ ∫Ω |∇u|p

Optimal insulation and free boundaries

We study models in which the unknown is a boundary layer or a free boundary: how to insulate or reinforce a body in the best way, and when an overdetermined condition forces a domain to be a ball.

  • Thermal insulation and reinforcement
  • Robin conditions and thin boundary layers
  • Bernoulli-type free boundary problems
  • Overdetermined problems of Serrin type
−Δu = 1 in Ω,  u = 0,  |∇u| = c on ∂Ω
⇒  Ω is a ball
